About My Research

I am a neuroscientist and linguist investigating the neurobiological and cognitive mechanisms underlying psychopathology across affective and psychotic disorders. My research takes a transdiagnostic and longitudinal perspective, integrating multimodal neuroimaging, detailed clinical phenotyping, computational linguistics, and machine learning to better understand symptom dimensions, individual differences, and the course of mental disorders. A particular focus of my work is on speech and language as quantitative markers of psychopathology, especially formal thought disorder. By combining acoustic, linguistic, and semantic features of natural speech with neuroimaging and clinical data, I aim to identify how language reflects underlying brain function, symptom severity, and clinical change. More broadly, I am interested in developing dynamic, biologically informed markers that can improve the characterization and prediction of psychiatric illness. I am a Principal Investigator within the Colloborative Research Center CRC/TRR 393 “Trajectories of Affective Disorders", where I co-lead the Information Infrastructure project, supporting large-scale, multisite research through data integration, quality assurance, and data sharing. My scientific work also draws on deeply phenotyped longitudinal cohorts, including FOR2107, to investigate shared and disorder-specific mechanisms across diagnostic boundaries. Alongside my research, I am committed to interdisciplinary teaching and mentoring, supervising students and early-career researchers across medicine, psychology, neuroscience, and related disciplines. My overarching goal is to bridge clinical phenomenology, neurobiology, and computational approaches to advance a more precise, individualized, and dynamic understanding of mental disorders.
