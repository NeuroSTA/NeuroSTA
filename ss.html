Svenja Seuffert

Research Assistant & PhD Researcher
Department of Psychiatry and Psychotherapy
Philipps-Universität Marburg, Germany
ORCID: 0009-0007-2230-4741

I am a PhD student at Philipps-Universität Marburg, working at the intersection of computational psychiatry, language research, and clinical neuroscience. My research examines how the way people speak relates to cognition, psychopathology, and brain structure, particularly in affective and psychotic disorders.

A central focus of my work is the computational analysis of spontaneous speech. I use natural language processing and acoustic analysis to characterize linguistic patterns that are difficult to capture through conventional clinical assessments alone. By combining these measures with detailed clinical and cognitive assessments, structural and diffusion MRI, and statistical and machine-learning methods, I aim to better understand both shared and distinct dimensions of psychiatric disorders. I am particularly interested in how speech can provide a complementary perspective on formal thought disorder and transdiagnostic variation in mental health.

Research Interests

Speech and language: Natural language processing, acoustic speech analysis, linguistic complexity and coherence, formal thought disorder.
Clinical neuroscience: Transdiagnostic psychopathology, cognition, structural and diffusion MRI.
Computational methods: Statistical modeling, machine learning, multimodal data integration.
